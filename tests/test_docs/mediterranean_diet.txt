Mediterranean Cuisine Overview
Origin and Culture
Mediterranean cuisine encompasses the traditional foods of countries bordering the Mediterranean Sea, including Greece, Italy, Spain, Morocco, Turkey, and Egypt. The diet emerged from ancient civilizations over thousands of years. UNESCO recognized the Mediterranean diet as an Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2010. The food pyramid places whole grains at the base and red meat at the top.

Core Ingredients
Olive oil is the primary fat source, particularly extra virgin olive oil. Tomatoes feature prominently in sauces and salads. Whole grains are preferred over refined grains. Legumes include chickpeas, lentils, and white beans. Nuts and seeds add healthy fats and protein. Fresh fruits and vegetables are consumed daily.

Key Dishes
Hummus is a chickpea-based spread originating from the Middle East. Greek salad combines tomatoes, cucumbers, olives, and feta cheese. Paella is a Spanish rice dish with seafood originating from Valencia. Moussaka is a Greek casserole with eggplant and ground meat. Pasta e Fagioli is an Italian soup with beans. Couscous is a North African grain dish.

Health Benefits
Studies show the Mediterranean diet reduces cardiovascular disease risk by 30%. It is associated with lower rates of type 2 diabetes. The diet may reduce risk of Alzheimer's disease by 23%. Olive oil consumption is linked to reduced inflammation. Omega-3 fatty acids from fish support brain health.

Wine Consumption
Moderate red wine consumption is traditional with meals, typically 1-2 glasses daily. Red wine contains resveratrol, an antioxidant compound. Wine consumption during meals is common in Mediterranean cultures. However, modern health guidelines note no safe level of alcohol consumption.

Cooking Techniques
Olive oil is used generously for cooking, drizzling, and dressings. Grilling is common for fish and vegetables. Slow-cooking stews develop deep flavors. Fresh herbs include basil, oregano, rosemary, thyme, and parsley. Lemon juice brightens flavors. Sea salt is preferred over processed salt.

Bread and Grains
Bread is eaten at most meals in Mediterranean cultures. Italian focaccia is a flatbread topped with olive oil and herbs. Whole grain sourdough is preferred over white bread. Rice dishes include risotto from Italy and paella from Spain. Bulgur is used in tabbouleh from Lebanon.

Seafood Traditions
Fresh fish is consumed 2-3 times weekly on average. Octopus is popular in Greek and Spanish cooking. Sardines and anchovies are common small fish. Swordfish and tuna feature in Italian cuisine. Shellfish including mussels, clams, and shrimp are widely used.

Meat Consumption
Red meat is limited to a few times per month. Poultry is consumed more frequently than red meat. Lamb is traditional in Greek and Middle Eastern cuisines. Pork features in Italian and Spanish cooking. Processed meats are rare in traditional Mediterranean diets.

Cheese Varieties
Feta cheese from Greece is made from sheep or goat milk. Mozzarella originated in southern Italy. Parmesan or Parmigiano-Reggiano is aged up to 36 months. Manchego comes from Spain. Halloumi from Cyprus can be grilled without melting.

Desserts and Sweets
Baklava is a pastry with nuts and honey from Turkey and Greece. Tiramisu is an Italian coffee-flavored dessert. Greek yogurt with honey is a traditional sweet. Fresh fruit is the most common dessert. Gelato is Italian-style ice cream with less fat than regular ice cream.

Beverages
Coffee is typically espresso-style in Italy and Greece. Turkish coffee is thick and served in small cups. Herbal teas including mint and chamomile are common. Water is consumed generously throughout the day. Fresh fruit juices are popular breakfast beverages.
