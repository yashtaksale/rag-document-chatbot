Space Exploration History
Mercury Program
NASA's Mercury program ran from 1958 to 1963. Alan Shepard became the first American in space in May 1961 aboard Freedom 7. John Glenn orbited Earth three times in February 1962. The program cost approximately $2.7 billion.

Gemini Program
The Gemini program operated from 1962 to 1966 with 10 crewed missions. It focused on developing spacewalk techniques. Astronaut Ed White performed America's first spacewalk during Gemini 4 in June 1965.

Apollo Program
The Apollo program achieved President Kennedy's goal of landing on the Moon before 1970. Apollo 11 landed Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on the lunar surface on July 20, 1969. Twelve people have walked on the Moon across six successful Apollo missions.

Apollo 13
Apollo 13 launched on April 11, 1970. An oxygen tank explosion forced the crew to abort the lunar landing. The crew returned safely to Earth on April 17, 1970. The mission is considered a successful failure.

Space Shuttle
The Space Shuttle program operated from 1981 to 2011, spanning 135 missions. The fleet included five orbiters: Columbia, Challenger, Discovery, Atlantis, and Endeavour. The Challenger disaster occurred in January 1986 and Columbia in February 2003.

International Space Station
The ISS has been continuously occupied since November 2000. It orbits Earth at an altitude of approximately 400 kilometers. The station has a pressurized volume of about 900 cubic meters and weighs 420 tonnes. Sixteen countries collaborated on its construction including USA, Russia, Japan, Canada, and 11 European nations.

Mars Rovers
Opportunity rover operated on Mars for 15 years from 2004 to 2018, traveling 45 kilometers. Curiosity has been exploring Gale Crater since August 2012. Perseverance, which landed in February 2021, is collecting rock samples for future return to Earth. Perseverance's companion helicopter Ingenuity completed 72 flights before retirement.

Voyager Probes
Voyager 1 launched in September 1977 and entered interstellar space in 2012. Voyager 2 also launched in 1977 and reached interstellar space in 2018. Both probes carry the golden record with sounds and images representing Earth civilization.

James Webb Telescope
JWST launched on December 25, 2021, aboard an Ariane 5 rocket. It orbits the Sun near the L2 Lagrange point, 1.5 million kilometers from Earth. The telescope's primary mirror has a diameter of 6.5 meters, consisting of 18 hexagonal segments.

Artemis Program
The Artemis program aims to return humans to the Moon by 2025 or later. Artemis 1 was an uncrewed test flight completed in December 2022. Artemis 2 will carry astronauts around the Moon but not land. Artemis 3 will perform the first crewed lunar landing since Apollo 17 in 1972.

Commercial Spaceflight
SpaceX has conducted over 300 Falcon 9 launches as of 2023. The company has achieved over 200 successful booster landings and reflights. Blue Origin has conducted multiple New Shepard tourism flights. The SpaceX Starship is the most powerful rocket ever built, with 100 tons of payload capacity to low Earth orbit.

Saturn
Saturn has 146 confirmed moons as of 2023. The planet is composed primarily of hydrogen and helium. Its iconic rings are made of ice and rock particles ranging from dust-sized to house-sized chunks.

Moon Facts
The Moon is 384,400 kilometers from Earth on average. Its surface gravity is one-sixth of Earth's. The same side of the Moon always faces Earth due to tidal locking. The Moon has no atmosphere and experiences extreme temperature variations.

Black Holes
Sagittarius A is the supermassive black hole at the center of the Milky Way. It has a mass equivalent to 4 million Suns. The first image of a black hole was captured in 2019, showing M87 53 million light-years away.
